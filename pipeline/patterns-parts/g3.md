## Group 3 observations
- rhythm: two clusters. Cut-driven pieces run median shots 53-237f with fast outliers (evanplace_A_eventbrite fastest 21f, evanplace_A_web-momentum fastest 40f @60fps=20f@30fps, B_trippy shots land at f34/86/141/177/229). Long-take pieces post huge medians (Kirschberg_B_yummy median 722f, Kirschberg_A_new-site slowest single shot 562f) because "shots" there are narrative subdivisions of one uncut take, not real cuts.
- structural: 4 of 12 are true continuous-take (cut_count ≤2: evanplace_B_openai-logo=1, _figured-out-figma=1, _had-a-lot-of-fun=1, _had-the-privilege=2); 2 more approach it with very long uncut spans inside a low cut_count (Kirschberg_B_yummy=3, cuts at f790/869 only; evanplace_A_8-trailers=3, chrome locked 161f+290f either side of one hard cut); the remaining 6 are cut-driven (5-18 cuts: B_trippy, Kirschberg_A_massive, Kirschberg_A_new-site, evanplace_A_eventbrite, _making-effort, _web-momentum).
- longform: the 29s (Kirschberg_A_new-site) and 53s (Kirschberg_B_yummy) pieces both hold attention via information/event density inside held shots rather than cut rate — new-site's 562f mosaic shot swaps every cell on its own staggered ~20f clock; yummy-yummy packs a title, an abstract orbit visualization, a live chat/typing beat, an agent-status swap, and a mockup-fan build into one 790f take before its first hard cut, changing "register" (per its own rhythm note) every ~15-20s rather than every few seconds. The ~20s pieces (_had-a-lot-of-fun, _had-the-privilege) get the same effect cheaper: a repeatable build/collapse-to-point cycle and a single locked camera with a new mechanical event every 24-40f, respectively — short pieces have no equivalent "new event on a clock inside one uncut shot" mechanism.
- gaps: none of the 12 show a pricing/plan comparison, a line/bar data-viz proving growth or ROI (only discrete counters, never a chart), multi-user/collaborative simultaneous cursors, or a competitor/before-after split-screen — all standard product-launch proof points absent from this group's corpus.
